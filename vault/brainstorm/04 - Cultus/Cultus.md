# Cultus - Mod Architecture Knowledge Base

## Overview

Cultus is the decorative and architectural materials mod of the [[Ars Mundi]] ecosystem.

Name:

Cultus

Pronunciation:

CUL-tus

Meaning:

Cultus comes from the Latin concept of:

- cultivation
- refinement
- care
- improvement
- ornamentation
- appreciation

The name represents the purpose of this mod:

> Transforming valuable materials into expressions of creativity and construction.

Cultus is not about progression.

It is not about power.

It is about allowing the player to use rare and meaningful materials to create beautiful structures.

---

# Role in Ars Mundi

Ars Mundi is divided into different disciplines.

Each mod represents a specific area of mastery.

Cultus represents:

> The discipline of architecture, decoration, and artistic expression.

[[Materia]] provides:

- gems
- exotic materials

Cultus transforms those materials into:

- decorative blocks
- building components
- architectural elements

Cultus answers:

> How can the materials of the world become part of the player's creations?

---

# Core Philosophy

## Materials Should Have Aesthetic Value

In vanilla Minecraft, many valuable materials have limited decorative usage.

Examples:

- diamonds are mostly equipment materials
- emeralds are mostly trading materials
- amethyst has limited decorative blocks
- rare materials often become hidden inside tools or machines

Cultus expands the idea that valuable materials should also have visual identity.

A material should not only make the player stronger.

It should also allow the player to express creativity.

---

# Cultus Is Not a Technology Mod

Cultus does not contain:

- machines
- processors
- automation
- energy systems
- production chains

The purpose is purely creative construction.

The player is not processing materials.

The player is displaying them.

---

# Cultus Is Not a Building Automation Mod

Cultus provides blocks.

It does not provide:

- construction machines
- builders
- automated placement
- structural systems

Those concepts belong elsewhere.

Cultus focuses on the blocks themselves.

---

# Material Philosophy

Cultus follows the material identity established by Materia.

Each decorative set represents the personality of its source material.

The material is not replaced.

It is expanded.

---

# Design Philosophy

## Complete Building Language

Cultus does not add only blocks.

It adds a complete architectural vocabulary.

For every material:

The player receives:

- surfaces
- transitions
- openings
- boundaries
- interactive elements

This allows the material to become a complete building style.

# Ars Vitae Relationship

Cultus is a creative extension of [[Ars Vitae]]

While other Ars Vitae mods improve survival:

- Arma improves capability
- Lorica improves protection
- Peram improves mobility
- Cista improves organization

Cultus improves expression.

It allows the player to build a world that reflects their achievements.

# Ars Mundi Relationship

In the complete Ars Mundi ecosystem, Cultus represents the final purpose of mastery:

Not only surviving the world.

Not only controlling the world.

# Final Philosophy

Cultus represents the transformation of resources into art.

A rare material should not disappear into a crafting table.

It should become part of the world.

The player gathers materials.

The player masters materials.

The player builds with materials.
