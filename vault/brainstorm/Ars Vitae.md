# Ars Vitae - Ecosystem Architecture Knowledge Base

## Overview

**Ars Vitae** ("The Art of Life") is the survival-focused subset of the Ars Mundi ecosystem.

Ars Vitae represents the disciplines required for a player to survive, adapt, explore, and thrive in the Minecraft world without introducing industrial technology.

It is designed for players who want:

- improved tools
- better armor
- advanced storage
- useful personal utilities
- improved interaction with the world

without adding:

- machines
- automation
- energy systems
- industrial processing
- transport networks

The philosophy of Ars Vitae is:

> The player masters survival through knowledge, preparation, and refined tools.

Ars Vitae is not a magic mod.

It does not use:

- spells
- mana
- wizard systems
- fantasy classes

Instead, it provides advanced items and systems that may feel magical because they exceed normal Minecraft limitations.

Examples:

- Tools that remove large amounts of blocks efficiently.
- Storage that exceeds vanilla limitations.
- Backpacks linked to advanced storage.
- Armor with enhanced abilities.
- Utilities that remove repetitive survival tasks.

The player is not using magic.

The player is using mastery.

---

# Ars Vitae Philosophy

## Survival Without Industry

Ars Vitae focuses on improving the player's relationship with the world.

The player should still:

- gather resources
- explore
- build
- mine
- interact with nature

However, unnecessary repetition should be reduced.

The goal is not to make resources infinite.

The goal is to make the player spend less time performing boring tasks and more time creating and exploring.

---

# Progression Philosophy

## Early Game

Early progression should still require effort.

Players must:

- gather materials
- craft basic equipment
- explore resources
- invest time before reaching advanced abilities

Powerful tools and equipment should feel earned.

---

## Mid Game

The player gains improved capability.

Examples:

- stronger tools
- better storage
- improved mobility
- specialized equipment

The player becomes more efficient.

---

## Late Game

The player reaches a state of mastery.

Examples:

- Large construction becomes easier.
- Resource collection becomes faster.
- Survival problems become manageable.
- Exploration becomes more rewarding.

The player is still playing survival, but with fewer limitations.

---

# Dependency Philosophy

Materia is the foundation of Ars Vitae.

Every Ars Vitae module depends on Materia.

This is intentional.

Materia provides:

- materials
- components
- shared resources
- common tags
- universal ingredients

# Materia

## Meaning

Materia represents elements, matter, and substance.

## Purpose

Materia is the material foundation of Ars Vitae.

It answers:

> What things are made from?

It does not define:

> How things are manufactured.

---

## Contents

### Exotic Materials

Example:

## Exodium

Exodium is an exotic End-related material.

It represents:

- unusual properties
- End-related behavior
- advanced potential

It is not simply a stronger tier of material.

It represents something different.

---

## Functional Alloys

Alloys represent properties rather than technology progression.

### Signalum

Signal-related alloy.

Used for:
- redstone-related systems

### Resistant Alloy

Durability and containment-focused alloy.

Used for:
- resistant containers
- advanced survival equipment

### Vibrant Alloy

Active and dynamic alloy.

Used for:
- advanced tools and systems

### Energetic Alloy

Energy-related material.

Primarily belongs to Ars Mundi systems.

Not required by Ars Vitae.

### Exodium Alloy

Advanced exotic alloy.

Primarily belongs to Ars Mundi systems.

### Thermium

Heat-related alloy.

Primarily belongs to Ars Mundi systems.

---

## Components

Materia provides universal material forms.

These represent material states:

- Gear
- Rod
- Plate
- Reinforced Plate
- Dust
- Nugget

Materia intentionally avoids:

- machines
- processors
- circuits
- industrial components

Those belong to Ars Mundi technology modules.

# Arma

## Meaning

Arma means tool or instrument.

## Purpose

Arma is the tool discipline of Ars Vitae.

It contains:

- swords
- shovels
- axes
- hoes
- pickaxes
- hammers
- specialized tools

It does not contain:

- armor
- machines
- energy systems
- industrial processing

---

## Design Goal

Arma tools eventually become extremely powerful.

Examples:

- faster harvesting
- instant tree processing
- advanced mining
- tunnel excavation
- special abilities

They may appear magical, but they represent advanced tools.

---

# Lorica

## Meaning

Lorica means armor or protective equipment.

## Purpose

Lorica provides player protection systems.

Contents:

- helmets
- chestplates
- leggings
- boots

Lorica is separated from Arma because:

Tools and protection represent different gameplay identities.

Future armor abilities may interact with Virtus from Ars Mundi.

---

# Peram

## Meaning

Peram represents a bag or pouch.

## Purpose

Peram provides portable storage.

Contents:

- normal backpacks
- Ender-linked backpacks

The Ender backpack connects with vanilla Ender Chest concepts.

The purpose is to improve player mobility and organization.

---

# Cista

## Meaning

Cista means chest or container.

## Purpose

Cista provides advanced storage blocks.

Contents:

- improved chests
- storage containers

Features:

- contents survive block breaking
- larger capacity than vanilla storage
- future expansion possibilities

Cista represents preservation and organization.

---

# Praxis

## Meaning

Praxis means action, practice, application.

## Purpose

Praxis provides player-focused utilities.

Examples:

- player teleporters
- entity teleporters
- infinite water bucket
- personal convenience tools

Praxis is about direct player capability.

It does not represent machines.

---

# Mutare

## Meaning

Mutare means to change, transform, alter.

## Purpose

Mutare provides world interaction utilities.

These are not machines.

They modify the world through special behaviors.

Examples:

## Chunk alteration utilities

A block may:

- clean a chunk
- remove unwanted blocks
- preserve valuable resources

## Fluid generation utilities

Examples:

- controlled water generation
- controlled lava generation

Mutare represents controlled world manipulation.

---
